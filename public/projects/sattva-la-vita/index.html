The case for it

Row House vs Apartment vs Building Your Own

vs a big apartment

Your own entrance, garden and terrace, no lift queues, and no neighbours above or below you. You'll pay for more built area, but you get ground under your feet.

vs an independent house

No plot hunting, approvals or contractors to manage. Security, landscaping and the clubhouse are handled for you.

vs a large villa project

63 homes is small enough that neighbours actually know each other, and far closer to the city than most villa communities.

In depth

Living in a La Vita Row House

A basement changes everything

Most row houses park cars on the street or in a front setback. La Vita's homes park two cars in their own basement, so the ground floor can open fully onto the garden. The basement also holds an entertainment room that owners use as a home theatre, gym or study, plus a staff room with its own toilet.

A lift makes four floors liveable

Four levels of stairs is the usual reason families, especially those with elderly parents, rule out row houses. Here a lift runs from the garage to the top-floor suite, so grandparents can take the ground-floor bedroom or any other floor and still reach the whole house.

Facing options for Vastu-minded buyers

Plans exist for east-, north- and west-facing homes, each with its room arrangement adjusted to the direction. If Vastu matters to your family, ask which facings remain before falling for a particular plan.

Nearly finished

Construction was reported at about 78% in February 2026, with RERA possession in late 2026. Unlike most Sattva launches, you can walk through near-complete homes before buying. See the timeline below for the latest site photos.

Light from above

Row houses share side walls, so light can only come in from the front and back. La Vita's plans counter this with a double-height cut-out over the living area and skylights over the kitchen and bathroom ducts, which bring daylight into the middle of the house.

Hennur's school belt

Hennur and Byrathi have become popular with families largely because of the international schools nearby. For parents, a row house here can mean short school runs and a garden, in one move.

On the site visit

What to Inspect in a Near-Finished La Vita Home

Buying this late in construction means you can check the actual build, not just a brochure. These are the parts of a four-level row house that are hardest to fix after you move in.

Below ground

  • Damp patches or seepage on basement walls
  • Where the sump and drainage pumps sit, and who maintains them
  • Ramp slope and turning room for your actual cars

Up top

  • Terrace waterproofing and slope towards the drains
  • Seals around the skylights and double-height cut-out
  • Railing height on the terrace and balconies

Inside

  • Lift make, warranty and the cost of the yearly maintenance contract
  • The specification list attached to your agreement, item by item
  • Stair width and headroom, if you're moving large furniture

Budgeting

Costs to Expect on a La Vita Row House

On top of the price

  • GST at 5% while under construction (none once the OC is issued)
  • Stamp duty roughly 5–5.6% and registration 1%
  • Clubhouse, maintenance deposit and corpus
  • Interiors: four floors and a basement is a lot of house to furnish

Timing can save GST

If the occupancy certificate is issued before you register, the sale is GST-free. On a row house at this price level, that can be a saving of many lakhs. Ask where the OC stands before you book.

After you move in

Running a Four-Level Row House

The community maintenance charge covers shared roads, security, landscaping and the clubhouse. Everything inside your own plot is yours to run, and a row house has more of it than an apartment.

Costs an apartment owner doesn't have

  • Private lift: a yearly maintenance contract after the warranty runs out
  • Garden and terrace: upkeep for the front and back gardens and the terrace planters
  • Waterproofing: resealing the terrace every few years
  • Basement: pump servicing and power for lighting and ventilation
  • Housekeeping: four floors take real effort to clean, which is what the staff room is for

Budget for it upfront

None of these is large on its own, but together they make a row house cost more to run than an apartment of the same area. Ask Sattva for the lift warranty period and the projected community charge per square foot, then add your own allowance for the items inside the house.

Sattva La Vita: Detailed Questions

Is La Vita an apartment project?
No. It's 63 four-bedroom row houses, each with its own basement, ground floor and two upper floors.
How far along is construction?
Public listings reported about 78% completion in February 2026, and the June 2026 site update showed finishing work such as basement tiling under way. Ask us for the latest progress before you visit.
Which schools are near La Vita?
Stonehill International, Canadian International, Vidyashilp Academy and Ryan International are all within a short drive.
Are home loans available?
Yes, from banks that have approved the project. Lenders treat row houses like apartments for loan purposes. For loans above ₹75 lakh, banks lend at most 75% of the property's value, so plan for a down payment of at least a quarter.
Can I customise the interiors?
With construction well advanced, structural changes aren't possible, but finishes and fit-out are yours to choose after handover.

Is it you?

Who La Vita Suits, and Who It Doesn't

A good fit if you

  • Live with three generations and need four bedrooms on separate floors
  • Want a garden and terrace but not the work of building a house
  • Have children in or headed for the Hennur and Thanisandra schools
  • Want to move in within months, not years
  • Need room for live-in help

Probably not for you if you

  • Are buying mainly for rental yield, where apartments let more easily
  • Want to resell quickly, as the row house resale market is thin
  • Commute daily to the south or east of the city
  • Prefer to hand all upkeep to an association
  • Need a published price before you'll even visit

Our honest take

Should You Buy at Sattva La Vita?

La Vita offers something rare near the city: a gated row house with its own basement garage, lift and garden, almost complete, in Hennur's school belt.

It's a home for a large family to live in, not a yield play. If that's what you need, the late stage of construction is a real advantage: you can inspect what you're buying, and if the OC lands before you register, you skip GST. Get the price sheet, visit in the facing you want, and work out the full cost of buying and running the house before deciding.

The biggest reasons to pause
