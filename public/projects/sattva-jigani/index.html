In depth

Why a 31-Floor Project in Jigani Is Different

Jigani has grown up as a place where people work rather than live. Its residential stock is mostly independent houses, small builder flats and plotted layouts serving the factories and tech campuses. Sattva's project changes the format, and that has knock-on effects for buyers, good and bad.

Height means views that stay

At about 100 metres, the upper floors here look over a largely low-rise neighbourhood. That makes views towards Bannerghatta's forest edge and Hennagara Lake unusually secure, at least until other high-rises arrive.

In most Bengaluru suburbs, a mid-floor view lasts until the next project goes up across the road. Here, the surrounding zoning and land use make that less likely in the near term, though not impossible.

Low density for a high-rise

About 432 homes on 6.37 acres works out to roughly 68 homes per acre. That's well below many airport-corridor projects (Aeropolis is around 91) and means fewer people sharing each lift, pool and lawn.

Three towers rather than eight or ten also means a smaller, quieter community, with a clubhouse sized to match.

A workforce on the doorstep

HCL's campus is directly behind the site, and the Jigani and Bommasandra industrial areas, Electronic City and Hosur Road's tech parks are all within a short drive. For people working there, a branded high-rise nearby has been hard to find.

That's also the rental case: tenants who'd otherwise commute in from HSR, BTM or Electronic City itself.

The trade-off: the area is still catching up

Jigani's roads, shops and schools were built for a smaller, mostly industrial population. Supermarkets, international schools and good restaurants are mostly a drive away, in Electronic City or Bannerghatta Road.

That will change as more housing arrives, but buyers moving from established neighbourhoods should visit at a weekday peak to see daily life here as it is now.

Carpet vs super built-up

How Much of Each Home You Actually Use

Carpet area is the floor inside your walls. Super built-up adds a share of walls, corridors, lifts and amenities. The ratio between the two, called efficiency, tells you how much you're really paying for.

Budgeting

A Worked Example: the Jigani 2 BHK

How the bill builds up

  • Base price: about ₹1.52 Cr for the 1,377 sq ft 2 BHK (indicative pre-launch)
  • Developer add-ons: car park, clubhouse, infrastructure, legal, generator, advance maintenance and corpus. On similar Sattva launches these run to several lakh
  • Floor rise: usually charged per sq ft per floor above a set level; significant in a 31-floor tower
  • GST: 5% of the agreement value for an under-construction home
  • Stamp duty and registration: roughly 5–5.6% plus 1% in Karnataka

Rough all-in figure

Putting those together, a ₹1.52 Cr base price is likely to become roughly ₹1.75–1.8 Cr by registration, depending on floor and the final charges at launch.

Daily life

A Realistic Picture of Living in Jigani

Mornings

If you work at HCL or in the industrial area, you could walk or take a short drive. Shift changes at the factories create traffic peaks at odd hours, so learn the local rhythm before relying on a commute estimate.

Shopping & eating out

Groceries and basics are available locally. For malls, larger supermarkets and a wider choice of restaurants, most residents head to Electronic City or Bannerghatta Road, 20–35 minutes away.

Weekends

Bannerghatta National Park and its biological park are close, and lakes such as Hennagara are nearby. Hosur, just across the state border, is an easy drive for shopping.

Schools

Schools serving Jigani and Anekal are local; many families with older children choose schools in Electronic City or on Bannerghatta Road. Factor school runs into your daily plan.

Healthcare

Clinics and smaller hospitals are local. The large multi-speciality hospitals are on Bannerghatta Road and Hosur Road.

Getting to the city

The Yellow Line from Bommasandra runs through Electronic City to Silk Board and RV Road, linking with the Green Line for the rest of the city.

Market context

How Jigani's Housing Market Is Changing

From plots to towers

For years, Jigani's housing meant independent houses, small builder flats and plotted layouts priced well below Electronic City. Branded high-rises like Sattva's signal that developers now see demand for gated apartment living here, driven by the industrial and tech workforce.

What the price tells you

At around ₹10,800–10,920 per sq ft, Sattva Jigani is priced at a premium to most existing Jigani stock and closer to newer projects in established southern localities. You're paying for the brand, height, views and amenities, not for Jigani's current price level.

The metro factor

The Yellow Line to Bommasandra has made the south-eastern edge much better connected. Areas within a short drive of its stations have seen growing interest from buyers who work further into the city.

Supply ahead

If more branded high-rises follow, Jigani's amenities will improve, but so will competition at resale. Being early, with low density and protected views, is Sattva Jigani's main advantage.

Our honest take

Should You Buy at Sattva Jigani?

Sattva Jigani is a bet on an area in transition. It brings branded, high-rise living to a neighbourhood that has mostly had low-rise housing, and offers low density and long views in return.

The risks are the usual pre-launch ones, plus a location whose shops, schools and roads are still catching up. Its price per sq ft is already close to established southern localities, so you're paying today for what Jigani may become.

Our view: it suits people working in Jigani, Bommasandra or Electronic City who want a large, modern home near work, and long-term buyers who are comfortable with a pre-launch purchase. Anyone needing RERA certainty now should look at Forest Ridge.
