In depth

Why Sattva Is Selling Plots at Doddaballapur

Doddaballapur sits at the north-western edge of the Bengaluru metropolitan region. For decades it was known for textiles and handlooms; today it's being pulled into the city's orbit by the airport, new universities and the Satellite Town Ring Road. Ekatra is a bet that this pull will keep growing.

Land, not a flat

A plot gives you something an apartment can't: full ownership of the ground and freedom over what you build and when. You can put up a house now, hold the land and build in ten years, or never build and sell the plot on.

Land doesn't depreciate the way a building does. That's why many buyers here see Ekatra as a long-term store of value rather than a home to move into soon.

What a branded layout adds

Plenty of cheaper revenue sites exist around Doddaballapur. What Ekatra offers on top is a RERA registration, a single clean title from the developer, finished roads, underground utilities and a gated, secured layout.

It also has a recognisable name on the title chain, which makes resale and bank loans more straightforward. That premium shows up in the price per sq ft.

The infrastructure behind the layout

Internal roads range from 8 to 18 metres wide. Electrical and sanitary lines run underground, so there are no overhead cables or open drains along the streets. The whole layout is gated with round-the-clock CCTV.

These things are easy to overlook in a brochure but expensive to fix later. They're the main difference between a layout that ages well and one that doesn't.

Who's buying here

Three groups, mostly: Bengaluru families banking land for a future home; investors betting on the STRR and the north-west growth corridor; and local families from Doddaballapur and Yelahanka who want a modern gated address close to where they already live.

Each group should pick a different plot size, which we cover below.

Budgeting

The All-In Cost of an Ekatra Plot

What to add to the plot price

  • Stamp duty: roughly 5–5.6% (including surcharge and cess) for property above ₹45 lakh; lower slabs below
  • Registration: 1%
  • GST: usually none on the land itself, though some developers charge GST on development or infrastructure components. Check the cost sheet line by line
  • Developer charges: clubhouse membership, maintenance deposit, electricity and water connections
  • Khata transfer and local body fees after registration

Example: a 30×40 plot

₹1.03 Cr plot price + about ₹5.8 lakh stamp duty + ₹1 lakh registration + a few lakh in developer charges comes to roughly ₹1.12–1.15 Cr before you lay a single brick.

Building a typical 2,400 sq ft G+1 home on that plot can easily cost as much again, depending on finishes. Budget for both if you plan to build soon.

Building your home

From Plot to House: The Steps

If you plan to build, here's the sequence most owners follow once the sale deed is registered.

Khata in your name

Transfer the khata after registration. Banks and approving authorities need it.

Design within the rules

Hire an architect who works within local setback and FAR rules, plus Ekatra's own building guidelines.

Plan approval

Submit drawings to the local authority and get the building plan sanctioned before work starts.

Build & connect

Appoint a contractor, build, then apply for permanent power and water connections.

Composite loans

A plot-plus-construction loan funds both stages, but most banks require construction to start within a set period after buying the plot.

Is there a deadline to build?

Some layouts require owners to build within a few years. Ask Sattva whether Ekatra has such a rule before you plan to hold the land long-term.

Design consistency

Layouts often control things like boundary walls, elevation colours and the number of floors. Ask for the guidelines early so your design isn't rejected.

Is a 20×30 plot worth buying?
It's the cheapest way into the layout and easy to resell to other budget buyers. It suits a compact home or a long-term land holding, but is tight for a large family house.

Master plan

Reading the Ekatra Layout

Main avenues vs local streets

The widest roads (up to 18 m) carry traffic through the layout; plots on 8–12 m streets are quieter and often better for families.

Corner and odd plots

Irregular plots at bends and edges can be good value if the shape suits your design. Corners add frontage and light but cost more.

Clubhouse placement

Plots close to the clubhouse are convenient but busier. Further in, streets are quieter.

Underground services

With power and sanitary lines underground, there are no poles or open drains along the streets, a cleaner look that also helps resale.
