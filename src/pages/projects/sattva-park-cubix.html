How we got here

Park Cubix, From Launch to Today

Park Cubix is one of the few Sattva projects on the airport side where you can trace a full cycle: launch, construction, handover, resale, then a second phase. That history is the most useful thing a buyer has, because it shows how the project has actually behaved rather than how a brochure says it will.

September 2017: launch

Sattva opens bookings at roughly ₹3,990 per sq ft. Devanahalli is still a mostly agricultural belt with the airport as its only real anchor.

2017–2022: construction

Basement + ground + 14 floor towers go up around a central courtyard on the 18-acre site, through the pandemic slowdown.

December 2022: Phase 1 handover

Owners start moving in. The clubhouse, pools and courts open, and the first resale and rental listings appear.

2025–26: Phase 2 and resale

Phase 2 is registered and selling at about ₹8,300–8,400 per sq ft, while Phase 1 flats trade between owners at roughly ₹6,890–8,050.

On the figures above, Phase 1 values have risen roughly 72% to 101% from the launch rate, depending on the unit. That's a gross figure, before stamp duty, interest and maintenance paid along the way, so the real return to an early owner is lower.

What living there is like

What Park Cubix Residents Talk About

Because Phase 1 has been occupied since late 2022, there's a body of resident feedback to read. Three issues come up repeatedly. None is unique to Park Cubix, but all three affect your monthly costs and comfort.

💧 Water supply

Residents report relying on tanker water rather than a piped municipal supply. That adds to maintenance costs and can be a worry in dry months. Ask the association what share of water comes by tanker today and what it costs per household.

⚡ Electricity tariff

Some owners say they're billed at commercial rather than domestic tariffs, which makes power noticeably dearer. Ask for a recent bill from a resident and check how metering works in the tower you're considering.

🌧️ Monsoon seepage

Seepage and waterproofing complaints appear in some reviews. Visit after rain if you can, look at ceilings and external walls in the specific flat, and ask whether the developer has carried out repairs.

What residents like: space

With 79% of the site left open and the towers placed around the edge, the central courtyard is genuinely big. Children's play, walking and community events all happen there.

What residents like: the clubhouse

Unlike many new launches where amenities are still promises, the clubhouse, pools and courts here are built and in daily use.

What residents like: the airport

A 12–15 minute run to the terminal is hard to beat. Frequent flyers and airport staff rate it highly for that alone.

What Each Phase 1 Layout Is Like to Live In

Phase 1 has five main layouts. Here's what owners and tenants tend to say about each, and what to check when you view one.

Studio (436–449 sq ft)

A single open room with a kitchenette and bathroom, designed for one person. Studios here are almost all rented out, often furnished, to airport and aerospace staff. When viewing, check natural light (inner-facing studios can be dim) and whether the kitchen has space for a fridge and washing machine.

1 BHK (658–742 sq ft)

The best-balanced investment format at Park Cubix: big enough for a couple, small enough to stay affordable, and the highest rental yield in the project. Larger 1 BHKs have a more usable living room; compare carpet areas, not just the headline size.

2 BHK, 2 bath (997–1,507 sq ft)

The range here is unusually wide. Smaller 2 BHKs are compact flats for small families; the largest are close to a 3 BHK in floor area. Always compare price per sq ft, and check whether the second bedroom can take a double bed.

3 BHK, 2 bath (from ~1,273 sq ft)

The most affordable way into a three-bedroom home near the airport. Two bathrooms for three bedrooms works for most young families, but can be tight with older children or live-in help.

3 BHK, 3 bath (up to ~1,498 sq ft)

The largest Phase 1 format. Attached bathrooms for every bedroom suit multi-generation households and make the flat easier to rent to sharing professionals.

Viewing tip

Visit two or three flats of the same type in different towers. Light, view, noise from the courtyard and distance to the lifts vary much more than the floor plan suggests, and they affect both comfort and resale.

Buying a resale

How a Park Cubix Resale Purchase Works, Step by Step

Buying from an owner is different from booking with Sattva. There's no RERA cover for the sale itself, so the checks matter more. Here's the sequence we follow with buyers.

Shortlist & view

We line up flats that fit your budget and format, and view them together, ideally including one after rain.

Document check

Sale deed, title chain back to Sattva, khata, tax receipts, the association's no-dues letter, and the seller's loan status.

Agreement & advance

A sale agreement fixes the price, timeline and what stays in the flat (fittings, appliances). An advance is paid against it.

Loan & valuation

Your bank values the flat and checks the title. If the seller has a loan, it's closed from your payment and the original documents released.

Registration & transfer

The sale deed is registered at the sub-registrar's office, then the khata, electricity account and association membership move to your name.

Typical timeline: four to eight weeks from agreement to registration, depending mainly on the bank and the seller's loan closure.

Master plan

Towers on the edge, a park in the middle

Park Cubix's layout is its defining feature. Instead of scattering towers across the site, Sattva placed the B+G+14 blocks around the perimeter of the 18 acres and left a large central courtyard open. That's where most of the 79% open space comes from.

The practical result: almost every flat faces either the courtyard or the outside of the site, and children can play in a big car-free space visible from many homes. Courtyard-facing flats get the better views and usually resell for more; outward-facing flats can be quieter at festival times.

When you shortlist, check which side your flat faces, how far it is from the clubhouse and gate, and whether it looks onto another tower at close range.

Getting the price right

How to Judge a Fair Resale Price at Park Cubix

Compare on a per-sq-ft basis

Phase 1 flats of the same type vary widely in size, so headline prices mislead. Convert every listing to price per sq ft of super built-up area, then adjust for floor, view and condition. The current spread of roughly ₹6,890 to ₹8,050 per sq ft is your reference range.

Price in the condition

A flat that needs waterproofing repairs, new fittings or repainting should be priced lower. Get a rough repair estimate before you negotiate, and use it.

Watch for "furnished" premiums

Sellers often ask extra for furniture and appliances. Value these at what they'd cost second-hand, not new, or ask for an unfurnished price.

Check what similar flats actually sold for

Registered sale values for recent transactions are a better guide than asking prices. We can help you find comparable recent sales.

Our honest take

Should You Buy at Sattva Park Cubix?

Park Cubix is the most honest-to-goodness value play in Sattva's airport-corridor line-up, and also the one that demands the most homework. It's the cheapest way to own a ready, branded flat within a quarter of an hour of the airport, and its courtyard plan gives it a sense of space that newer, denser projects lack.

But the discount isn't free money. Water supplied by tanker, reports of commercial electricity tariffs and monsoon seepage are the kinds of issues that raise your monthly costs and test your patience. They don't affect every flat equally, which is exactly why inspecting the specific unit, talking to residents and pricing in repairs matters so much here.

Our view: for an airport-side worker, a frequent flyer or a yield-focused investor buying a well-chosen 1 BHK at the lower end of the price range, Park Cubix can make a lot of sense. For a family that wants a hassle-free home for the long term, the newer Phase 2, or a project like Vasanta Skye, may be worth the extra cost and the wait.

Inspect the specific flat, ideally after rain, and get a recent maintenance bill and electricity bill from a resident of the same tower.
Can you help me negotiate a resale price?
Yes. We'll compare the flat with recent sales on a per-sq-ft basis, factor in condition and furnishing, and help you make a sensible offer.
How quickly do Park Cubix flats rent out?
Well-priced, furnished 1 BHKs and studios typically rent quickly to airport and aerospace staff. Larger homes can take longer.
