Layout plans

Reading the Kaveri Siri Layout

Two plots of the same size can differ a lot in price and in how pleasant they are to live on. These are the things we go through on the plan before your site visit.

What to look for on the plan

Facing. East- and north-facing plots usually cost more, mostly because of Vastu preference. West and south facings can be good value if that doesn't matter to you.

Road width in front. Roads here are 9 to 12 m. A plot on a 12 m road is easier for construction access and parking, and usually resells better.

Park-facing and corner plots. With six parks there are plenty of park-facing plots, and they carry a premium. Check whether a corner's extra frontage suits your house design before paying for it.

The bypass edge. Sattva's Phase II plan shows a proposed 60 m bypass road along one side. Plots near it will be easy to reach but noisier, so pick based on which matters more to you.

Budgeting

Costs to Budget For Beyond the Plot Price

The per-sq-ft rate is only part of the bill. Here's what usually comes on top, so the final number doesn't surprise you.

Government charges

Stamp duty and registration at Karnataka rates, payable when the sale deed is registered. The rate depends on the property value, so we'll work it out for your plot.

Developer charges

Typically clubhouse membership, a maintenance deposit and electricity and water connection charges. Go through the cost sheet line by line; we'll do it with you.

Plot loans

Banks lend a lower share of a plot's value than of a flat's, so plan for a bigger down payment. A composite plot-plus-construction loan works if you'll build soon. Ask which lenders have approved the project.

Payment schedule

Usually a booking amount, an agreement-stage payment and the balance at registration. Confirm whether Phase II is construction-linked or time-linked, and get the schedule in writing first.

Growth claims

You'll see promises of 20–30% appreciation online. Treat these as sales pitches. A better guide is what Phase I plots have actually resold for. Ask us for recent examples.

Where your money goes

All payments go directly to the developer's project account. As a channel partner we never collect booking money in our own name.

Building on your plot

From Kaveri Siri Plot to Finished House

Most buyers either build within a few years or hold the land. If you plan to build, here's the path and a rough sense of what it takes.

Registration & khata

Once the sale deed is registered, transfer the khata to your name. You'll need it for the plan approval and any construction loan.

Design

Work with an architect within the setback rules and Kaveri Siri's building guidelines. A 30×40 plot typically takes a ground + 1 or ground + 2 home.

Approval

Submit drawings to the local authority for a building plan sanction before work begins.

Construction

Choose a contractor. In Mandya, local labour and materials keep build costs below Bengaluru rates.

Connections

Apply for permanent electricity and water connections, then move in or rent out.

What a typical house might look like

On a 30×40 (1,200 sq ft) plot, a ground + 1 home of roughly 2,000–2,400 sq ft built-up is common: three bedrooms, a living and dining area, a car park and a small garden strip. On a 40×60, there's room for a villa with a lawn and four or more bedrooms.

Budgeting the build

Construction costs depend heavily on finishes and on whether you hire a turnkey builder or manage trades yourself. Get two or three quotes from contractors who've built in the layout. Ask Phase I owners who've already built, and we can help you get in touch.
